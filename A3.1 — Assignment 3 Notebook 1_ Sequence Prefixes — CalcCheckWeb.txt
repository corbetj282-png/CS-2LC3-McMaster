The preloaded “Replacement” theorems now include also the following, corresponding to inference rule (1.8) “Leibniz” in LADM:
“Leibniz” (3.83.1):


d = e ⇒ f d = f e


“Leibniz” (3.83.6):

d₁ = d₂ ∧ e₁ = e₂ ⇒ (p d₁ e₁ ≡ p d₂ e₂)

In addition you also have the following “Replacement” variant (with similar usage pattern, between explicit Substitution steps) available:
“Indirect replacement”:

(p ⇒ e = f) ⇒ (p ∧ E[z ≔ e] ≡ p ∧ E[z ≔ f])

(This has so far been seen under the name “Conditional replacement”.)
Note that for sequences, you have in particular
“Non-empty-sequence decomposition”:

xs ≠ 𝜖 ⇒ xs = head xs ◃ tail xs

from Ex6.1. Using “Indirect replacement” (from above) with this may be useful for proving the theorem below.

pre-postcondition specification:
