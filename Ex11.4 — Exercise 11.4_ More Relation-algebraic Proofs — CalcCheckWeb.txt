One simple consequence of any modal rule is the following theorem (also known as “co-difunctionality”); the name “Hesitation” is intended to
suggest that whenever you can make a step forward to some place, you can also first make a step forward, then one back, and finally another
one forward to the same place (due to the implicit assumption that the place you started from is still available for the backwards step after the
first step forward).


(To be able to apply the modal rule, you need to somehow convert the starting point R into an expression of shape “X ⨾ Y ∩ Z”. Idempotency

After assuming the antecedent, proving “Swapping mapping across ⊆” can proceed via a nice cyclic implication chain. It is useful to keep in
mind that
“Flipped Transitivity of ⊆”: R ⊆ S ⇒ Q ⊆ R ⇒ Q ⊆ S
is a monotonicity property for the operation that turns a relation X into the proposition (Q ⊆ X); this maps relation orderings “R ⊆ S” to
proposition orderings “(Q ⊆ R) ⇒ (Q ⊆ S)”.
Similarly,
Axiom “Transitivity of ⊆”: Q ⊆ R ⇒ R ⊆ S ⇒ Q ⊆ S
is an antitonicity property for the operation mapping a relation X to the proposition (X ⊆ S).
