Starting Abstract Relation Algebra from Inclusion, Composition, Identity, and Converse
[2]

This notebook is the first in a sequence on abstract relation algebra, which is concerned with reasoning about relations without descending to
the level of the related elements.
Therefore, the relationship notation _⦗_⦘_ is not available here, since that relationship notation applies in the setting of concrete relations, that is,
of
“a binary relation is a set of pairs”,
and we are not considering that setting here.
Instead, we build up a theory that talks only about relations, and not about the elements these might relation. This theory is called “abstract
relation algebra”.
That said, concrete relations do form a model of the theory developed under the label “abstract relation algebra”. In other words, concrete
relations, implemented as sets of pairs, and their operations satisfy the axioms presented here in abstract relation algebra, and therefore all the
theorems developed in the abstract setting are valid for concrete relations as well.

If a total relation is contained in a univalent relation, then both are equal.
In a proof like this, when thinking about inclusions of and in identity relations 𝕀, it is important to be aware of the typeing!
For example, in the following theorem “total in univalent”, relations R and S have to have the same type, e.g., A ↔ B, for types A and B that must
not be assumed to be the same.
Therefore, when totality of R means “𝕀 ⊆ R ⨾ R ˘”, this applies to the identity in “𝕀 ⨾ S”, but not to the identity in “S ⨾ 𝕀”.
