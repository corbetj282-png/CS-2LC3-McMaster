[1]

In Exercises 11.1 and 11.2 (and the Reference Notebooks “Abstract RA: Starting from Inclusion and Composition” and “Abstract RA:
Continuing with Converse”), we started the development of abstract relation algebra with the introduction of the inclusion order _⊆_ and the
converse ˘_ and composition _⨾_ operators.
Four hint items per hint are allowed here, so many simple proofs can be straight-forward ”By”-invocation. If you don’t “see” how these proofs
work, spell them out!
For practice, the keyword Monotonicity is disabled here.
Now we add an intersection operator, and with it the assumption that any two relations R and S of the same type A ↔ B have R ∩ S as their
greatest lower bound in the inclusion order:

For proving “Sub-distributivity of ⨾ over ∩”, the natural approach is to use an inclusion chain starting from the left-hand side with a reverse
“Idempotency of ∩” step, as shown in class.


You are used to CalcCheck performing multiple equational rewrites in a single calculation step; note that CalcCheck currently does not chain
several inclusion ⊆ steps in one calculation hint in a similar way. So in doubt, split your complex steps into simpler pieces, in particular
inclusion ⊆ steps.
