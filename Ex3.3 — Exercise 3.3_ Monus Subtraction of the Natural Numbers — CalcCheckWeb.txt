Monus (Subtraction)
[3]

Unlike the integers, which satisfy LADM Axiom (15.6) “Additive inverse” (∃ x : ℤ • x + a = 0 ), addition on the natural numbers has no
inverse. In particular, unary minus is not available on the natural numbers.
Under these circumstances, subtraction behaves differently, and subtraction with properties like those of the operator _-_ we are defining here is
also called more precisely monus.

In contexts where we have order available we will be able to prove:
m < n ⇒ m - n = 0
m ≥ n ⇒ (m - n) + n = m
In the second case, subtraction therefore coincides with subtraction on the embedding of ℕ in ℤ.

Each of the above two proofs will most likely only have used two of the subtraction axioms, and instantiating the single variable m via induction
always was sufficient to enable use of one of these axioms.
For “Subtraction after addition”, this is not the case anymore, and nested induction is needed; note that in nested induction steps, you have to
make it explicit which of the available induction hypotheses you are using, by writing:
Induction hypothesis `YourInductionHypothesisExpression`
Since induction proofs are just another kind of proofs (the main kind of proofs you have seen so far is calculations), they can go wherever a
proof can go — arbitrarily-deep nesting is possible.
