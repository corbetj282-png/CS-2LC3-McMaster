Be aware that metavariables like E and P here are intended to be instantiatable with arbitrary expressions.
All steps must remain valid not matter which expression such a metavariable is instantiated with.
Therefore, the following step is invalid:
E[z ≔ 2]
=⟨ Substitution ⟩
E
One instantiation that shows this is instantiating E with z + 1; the resulting instance of the step above is
clearly invalid:
(z + 1)[z ≔ 2]
=⟨ Substitution ⟩
z + 1
Therefore substitutions into metavariables, such as E[z ≔ a], cannot be simplified (unless a
“¬occurs(`z`,`E`)” proviso is in scope; this will be introduced with quantification).

The “Leibniz” axiom above is an implication; all the theorems derived from it in LADM Section 3.6 are
equivalences.
As far as these equivalences specialise the argument type of one or both of the equalities = in the
“Leibniz” axiom to 𝔹, LADM silently uses ≡ instead of =.
Since these two are treated as different in CalcCheck, additional conversion steps are required, using the
conversion theorem you already know:
“Definition of ≡”:

(p ≡ q) = (p = q)
