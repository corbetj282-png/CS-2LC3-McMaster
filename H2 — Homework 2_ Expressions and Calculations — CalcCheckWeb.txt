Mathematical Expressions
[7]

For a first definition of mathematical expressions, read also section 1.1 in LADM.
For the particular case we are looking at in this current notebook, expressions will be formed only
according to the following rules, which are special cases of the rules in LADM section 1.1.:
Each integer literal consisting of digits and at most one initial “-” (e.g., “70” or “2” or “-18”) can
be used as an expression.
Each variable name (below just single lower-case letters such as “x” or “a”) can be used as an
expression.
If E is an expression, then “(E)” is an expression.
If E is an expression, then “- E” is an expression.
Here, “-” is used as a unary prefix operator.
In CalcCheck, such prefix operators need to be followed by a space (except before parentheses).
If E₁ and E₂ are expressions, then “E₁ + E₂” and “E₁ - E₂” and “E₁ · E₂” are expressions.
Here, “+”, “-”, and “·” are used as binary infix operators.
Note that in CalcCheck, infix operators always have to be surrounded by spaces (except before
and after parentheses, but even there, spaces are still strongly recommended for readability).
(You type the multiplication symbol as “\.” or “\cdot”. It can never be omitted in this course.)
Note in particular that “-5” is a negative integer literal, while “- 5” is an application of unary minus to the
integer literal “5”.

As you considered the last two, you may have noticed that each of these can be generated from the rules
in two different ways, because:
Both “4 · a” and “3” are expressions, and also both “4” and “a + 3” are expressions.
Both “4 - a” and “3” are expressions, and also both “4” and “a + 3” are expressions.
You probably immediately discarded thinking of “4 · a + 3” as “4 · (a + 3)”, because you are
trained to recognise the multiplication operator “·” as having higher precedence than the addition
operator “+”. And if you look at the precedence table in the inside cover of the textbook (referred to from
section 1.1), then you see that this is also the case in LADM.
But what about “4 - a + 3”, and, similarly, “a - 3 - b”?
A sentence below the precedence table specifies in particular that the subtraction operator “-” associates
to the left, and this means that:
a - 3 - b

=

(a - 3) - b

In addition, the addition operator “+” and the subtraction operator “-” mutually associate to the left,
which means:
4 - a + 3

=

(4 - a) + 3

c + 5 - d

=

(c + 5) - d
