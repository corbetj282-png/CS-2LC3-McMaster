Preloaded here is the theory of integers including order essentially following LADM chapter 15.
On page 308 in LADM, which roughly corresponds to Ex4.4, there is a nameless theorem at the end of the box which actually does have a
conventional name: “Indirect equality”.
Indirect equality turns into an occasionally-useful proof method, and therefore is worth remembering: For proving an equality, one may instead
prove the universally-quantified equivalence of two order statements.
The natural way to prove indirect equality properties is Using mutual implication:

For the definition of the minimum and maximum operators on the integers, we essentially follow LADM section 15.2. An exact rendering of the
axioms in LADM would be:
-- Axiom (15.53) (15.53a) “Definition of ↓”:
-∀ z • z ≤ x ↓ y ≡ z ≤ x ∧ z ≤ y
-- Axiom (15.53) (15.53b) “Definition of ↑”:
-∀ z • x ↑ y ≤ z ≡ x ≤ z ∧ y ≤ z
By (9.16), the single universal quantifications used there do not make any difference. However, the different quantification status of x, y, and z is
not well-motivated. We could of course switch to quantifying all three variables:
---


∀ x, y • ∀ z • z ≤ x ↓ y ≡ z ≤ x ∧ z ≤ y
∀ x, y • ∀ z • x ↑ y ≤ z ≡ x ≤ z ∧ y ≤ z


However, it is much less cumbersome to just use the version without quantifiers (equivalent by (9.16) to the LADM version):
