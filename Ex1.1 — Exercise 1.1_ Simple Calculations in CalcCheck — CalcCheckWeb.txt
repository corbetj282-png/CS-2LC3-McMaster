For clarifying terminology, here is an example calculation step:
x + 2
=⟨ “Symmetry of +” ⟩
2 + x
The part between the hint brackets “⟨” and “⟩” is called the hint for this calculation step, and it serves to
justify that the expression before (x + 2) is related via the relation denoted by the calculation operator
(=) with the expression after (2 + x). So, altogether, in this case the hint “Symmetry of +” is expected to
justify the proposition x + 2 = 2 + x (and it does).
In general, a hint may contain several hint items. For now, only one hint item will be allowed per hint, and
the only kinds of hint you can use here are:


Theorem references: These can be
theorem names in pretty double quotes, such as “Unary minus”,
theorem numbers, which are always enclosed in parentheses, such as (15.5b)
combinations of both, which narrow down the selection of theorems to their intersection; this
is currently mostly useful to disambiguate while still documenting the proof via the theorem
name, as you can see below in the first hint:
“Distributivity of · over -” (15.29b)
In that particular case, omitting the number would still be accepted, but the system then may
explore the unused (15.29a) before it is successful with (15.29b).
Basic facts such as: Fact `5 · 6 = 30` . The expression between the backticks here has to
consist entirely of literals and operators for which built-in evaluation is enabled (which it is here for
the operators you have). In particular, expressions containing variables are not accepted for Fact;
writing Fact `x · 0 = 0` will only get you an unjustified step.
The keyword hint item Substitution, which performs substitutions, for example:
(x + 2)[x ≔ 1]
=⟨ Substitution ⟩
1 + 2
