Conjunction

[1]

This notebook follows LADM section 3.5 to introduce conjunction using the single axiom “Golden rule”.
As explained there, due to associativity of equivalence, this axiom has again many readings; the
“canonical reading” in the context here is:
Direct definition of conjunction: (p ∧ q) = (p ≡ q ≡ p ∨ q)
However, a few more readings will be used routinely:
Equivalence of two formulations of implication: (p ∧ q ≡ p) = (q ≡ p ∨ q)
One alternative formulation of equivalence: (p ≡ q) = (p ∧ q ≡ p ∨ q)

The golden rule is the only LADM axiom for conjunction.

With the golden rule, it is particularly easy to make CalcCheck time-out:
Most time-outs are due to incorrect steps!
Frequently, splitting your proof into smaller steps will either solve the problem, or make the
underlying error more obvious.
Supplying an explicit substitution via “ with `p, q ≔ … , …` ” should normally be sufficient to
resolve the problem — however, if such a step is not justified, CalcCheck may still run into a timeout.
If you have a hint that times out, then CalcCheck automatically inserts “ ? , ” at the beginning of
that hint — hints that contain ? are not checked, so as long as you leave that ? in that hint, it will not
give rise to time-outs anymore.


To try that out, you can try the following step instead of a proof for Theorem (3.36) “Symmetry
of ∧”:

q ∧ r
Therefore, if you have a hint that times out, and you are not working on fixing it, keep that “ ? , ”
prefix there to reduce load on the server.
Superfluous re-checking of hints that time out constitutes a denial of service attack, and, as
such, a violation of academic integrity.
Please avoid saving versions that contain time-outs!
