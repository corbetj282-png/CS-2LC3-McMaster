For two sets S and T, their union S ∪ T consists of those elements that occur in S or in T, whereas their intersection S ∩ T consists of only the
common elements.

Since these two operations are defined in-terms of the familar conjunction and disjunction, they also inherit their properties, see LADM
Metatheorem (11.25).
As long as we cannot use that metatheorem, we have to manually bridge the gap between set theory and propositional calculus; for set equalities,
the main tool for that is “Set extensionality”:
